1. **Sunlight enters Earth's atmosphere**: When sunlight enters the Earth's atmosphere, it consists of a wide range of colors, including red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, and violet. These colors make up white light.

These molecules are much smaller than the wavelength of visible light. According to Rayleigh's law of scattering, shorter (blue) wavelengths are scattered more than longer (red) wavelengths by the small molecules of the atmosphere.

3. **Blue light is scattered in all directions**: Because of its shorter wavelength, blue light is scattered in all directions by the molecules of the atmosphere. This scattering effect gives the sky its blue appearance.

4. **Why the sky appears blue during the day**: When you look at the sky, you are seeing the scattered blue light that has been dispersed in all directions. The blue color is more pronounced during the daytime because the sun is overhead, and the light has to travel through more of the atmosphere to reach your eyes.

5. **Why the sky appears more red during sunrise and sunset**: During these times, the sun's light has to travel through more of the Earth's atmosphere to reach your eyes. The longer wavelengths of light (reds and oranges) are not scattered as much as the shorter wavelengths (blues and violets). As a result, the red light is able to travel more directly to your eyes, giving the sky a reddish hue.

6. **Atmospheric conditions**: The color of the sky can also be affected by atmospheric conditions, such as dust, water vapor, and pollution. These particles can scatter light in different ways, altering the apparent color of the sky.

So, to summarize, the sky appears blue because of the scattering of sunlight by the tiny molecules of gases in the atmosphere, with the shorter wavelengths of light (blues and violets) being scattered more than the longer wavelengths (reds and oranges).
